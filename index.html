Permanent Collection — Personality Exhibits

Popsi Lore

Exhibit
001

The Laugh

It arrives without warning. In the middle of a serious conversation, during an argument, at the exact moment you think something heavy is coming — it appears. The laugh. Big, warm, completely disarming. Whatever was tense suddenly feels small. People in the room relax without knowing why. He has never once explained how he does it. He probably does not know.

Status: Fully Operational
Exhibit
002

The Nagging Protocol

It begins with a long message. Numbered. Detailed. Sent at a time you were not expecting it. It covers the issue you already know about, the issue you forgot about, and two issues that have not happened yet but might. It will follow up. It will ask if you read it. And here is the part that matters: he is almost always right. Not sometimes. Almost always. The nagging is not nagging. It is a man who has already thought three steps ahead, trying to save you the trouble of learning the hard way.

Running Since: 1974
Exhibit
003

Dad Telepathy

He texts at 1am. Not because anything is wrong — just to check. He will wait online past midnight and, when there is no reply, type "I guess you're asleep already 😞" and send a blessing anyway. He has called at the exact moment something was going wrong, before being told a single thing. There is no scientific explanation. There is only one theory: he never stopped paying attention.

Classification: Overpowered
Exhibit
004

The Research Department

Present a problem — any problem. He will not say "I don't know" and leave it there. He will investigate, return with a full breakdown, and end it with a recommendation and a prayer. He is not trying to impress anyone. He just cannot stand the idea of you being without a direction.

Powered by: Faith and Google
Exhibit
005

The Morning Blessing

It does not matter what time the conversation ended the night before. It does not matter how late things ran, or whether the network was bad, or whether the last message was an argument. The next morning, something arrives. A Bible verse. A prayer. A voice note. Sometimes just "Good morning, Inioluwa." It has not missed a single day. Not one. That kind of consistency is not habit — it is a choice, made every morning, before anything else.

Delivery: Every Morning. Without Exception.

Private Collection — Memory Vault

Moments We Remember

Memory — 001

That message at 1am — "Inioluwa go and sleep now" — sent from a man who had been awake, online, waiting for me since 10pm. I was the one who needed the rest. He was the one who could not sleep until he knew I was okay.

Memory — 002

"Are they on admission and scholarship too?" — the day I told him there were rats in my room at school and he turned it into a headline. Then immediately added: "Please don't show your Mum, otherwise I enter hot soup."

Memory — 003

"Sleep tight — and with your teeth, not your mouth, open." He said this at the end of a long, serious conversation about money and growing up. And then just... said that. That is the man entirely.

Memory — 004

I asked how he was doing. He replied: "Am doing gooder." No correction. Completely confident. I did not say a word. I just smiled and answered him.

Memory — 005

Power came back after a long outage. He sent: "I HAVE POWER!! PDP: Power to the boys. 💪" The excitement was completely real. I saved that message. That is the version of him I want to remember forever.

Personal Archive — A Son’s Words

Letter From Josiah

Written by — Josiah Inioluwa Odetayo · June 2026

Dear Popsi,

Popsi, I have been trying to figure out how to say this properly for a long time. Not because I do not know what I want to say — but because some things feel bigger than a phone call, bigger than a WhatsApp message, bigger than "thank you, sir." You have shown up for me in ways I did not always notice in the moment. The 1am messages. The morning verses. The long paragraphs of advice I sometimes read halfway and promised myself I would finish later. I always finished them. This is me finally saying, properly, what I should have said more often.

You have never once made your sacrifices feel like something I owe you. When money was short — and I know there were times it was very short — you still said "bear with me" instead of "I can't." You bought data in small amounts just to stay connected to me. You found ways to send what you had before it was even ready. And in the middle of all of that, you still made jokes. You still laughed. You still found something to be grateful about. I do not know how you do that. I am still learning how to do that.

You have a kind of faith I do not fully understand yet. Not the kind that sits and waits — the kind that moves. That researches. That plans. That says "God organized this" and then still wakes up the next morning and does the work. You told me once that where I am was not your power or ability — God organized it. I think about that more than you know. I want to carry things the way you carry them. Quietly. Without complaint. With the certainty that it will work out, even when the evidence is still thin.

I do not say thank you enough. Not for the money — though that matters — but for never disappearing. For being the kind of father I can actually talk to. For treating every problem I brought to you, even the careless ones, like they deserved your full attention at midnight. For every "God bless you, Inioluwa" you have ever sent without waiting to be asked. For raising me to be someone Daniel and Dorcas can look up to — and trusting me to be that, even when I was still figuring it out. Happy birthday, Popsi. Fifty-two years of showing up. I am proud — genuinely proud — to be your son.

Thank you for everything. Fifty-two years well lived. Many more ahead.

With love and respect,
Josiah.
